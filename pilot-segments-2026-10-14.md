# Pilot user proto-segments and JTBD — 2026-10-14

## Decision and evidence level

- Decision this segmentation informs: which of three information-work problems should be the first narrowly scoped pilot and prototype path.
- Evidence used: owner-provided examples; the aggregate pilot synthesis, which signals difficulty with overly general AI output and responsibility for errors but does not identify segment prevalence; methods from the segmentation, desk-research, and design-thinking learning materials.
- Unsupported assumptions: each listed segment is present in the target audience, frequent enough to prioritise, and addressable by the CoreTeams product. These are proto-segments, not validated personas or demand claims.
- Owner: project owner

## Segment 1 — the evidence reconciler

- Direction: reconcile conflicting or uneven information from several sources into one defensible working conclusion.
- Observable context: a person has multiple relevant sources and a task cannot progress until they choose an interpretation, recommendation, or next step.
- Inclusion criteria: completed or is completing a multi-source task; encountered conflicting, incomplete, or differently framed information; can reconstruct a recent case without revealing confidential material.
- Exclusion criteria: needs only a single factual lookup, or has no decision dependent on resolving the information.
- Pain points: cannot determine which source is sufficiently reliable; must manually compare contradictions; loses the trail from conclusion back to evidence; fears advancing an incorrect conclusion and being responsible for it.
- Job Story: **When** several sources give me incomplete or conflicting answers and I need a defensible next step, **I want to** compare the evidence, note confidence and unresolved disagreement, and form one working conclusion, **so I can** continue the task and explain why the decision is justified.
- Existing alternatives: manual notes or spreadsheets, repeated web search, asking a colleague or domain expert, choosing the most familiar source.
- Disconfirming question: in the last relevant task, was resolving disagreement actually a meaningful blocker, or did the task move forward without it? What evidence made the final answer trustworthy?

## Segment 2 — the fragmented-context assembler

- Direction: turn dispersed files and materials into one navigable, current working artefact.
- Observable context: a person receives or finds files in several places or formats and must form a shared picture before creating, presenting, or deciding something.
- Inclusion criteria: recently gathered at least three related files, links, or notes from more than one location or format; needed to produce a summary, brief, plan, presentation, or other shared artefact.
- Exclusion criteria: materials are already consolidated and versioned in a usable workspace, or the task is simple file storage rather than understanding.
- Pain points: incomplete collection and hidden duplicates; version confusion; time lost opening, reading, and re-summarising materials; loss of provenance and context when compressing information; no single artefact that another person can safely use.
- Job Story: **When** the information I need is distributed across files, links, and formats, **I want to** assemble it into one structured artefact that preserves source context and current status, **so I can** see the whole situation and hand off or continue work without repeating collection.
- Existing alternatives: folders and file names, shared drives, manual document merging, copy-paste into a brief, personal notes, asking the sender to clarify.
- Disconfirming question: did the last task actually fail or slow down because materials were fragmented, or was the real problem selection, analysis, approval, or access rights?

## Segment 3 — the expert-gap executor

- Direction: make a bounded, reviewable progression when context and a goal exist but qualified domain review is unavailable.
- Observable context: a person owns a task and has source materials and a desired outcome, but cannot obtain timely help from a subject-matter expert to decide the next quality step.
- Inclusion criteria: recently had a task with a clear objective and available context; lacked prompt access to a qualified expert; had to choose a workaround, defer, or deliver with uncertainty.
- Exclusion criteria: the task is legally, medically, financially, or otherwise high-stakes and cannot proceed without an authorised professional; an expert was readily available and was used.
- Pain points: does not know which gaps are critical; cannot judge whether a result is accurate enough; repeatedly reworks drafts after delayed feedback; progress stalls while waiting; bears responsibility without enough confidence.
- Job Story: **When** I have the context and a clear task but cannot reach a qualified expert in time, **I want to** turn the available information into a transparent draft with explicit gaps, checks, and escalation points, **so I can** make safe progress without pretending that an unverified answer is expert judgement.
- Existing alternatives: waiting for an expert, searching documentation and examples, asking non-expert colleagues, producing a low-confidence draft, narrowing or postponing the task.
- Disconfirming question: would a structured draft actually unblock the last task, or was authorised expertise indispensable? Which decision or check must remain with a professional?

## Consent and research boundary

- Before any session, explain the purpose, voluntary participation, ability to skip or stop, and that no names, contacts, raw files, recordings, or identifiable quotes are retained here.
- Ask about a completed or ongoing real case. Treat ideal-future answers as counterfactual expectations only; they cannot validate a barrier, demand, or product fit.
- Owner approval is required before identifying contacts, recruitment, invitations, or any personal-data collection.
