## Hypothesis and evidence boundary

- Product hypothesis: people who recently completed a complex information task that produced a structured artefact will report recurring, consequential friction in synthesising and validating information; a flow that makes those stages explicit and preserves the resulting artefact would be relevant to their actual workflow.
- Existing observed evidence: the owner-approved aggregate form signals overly general AI output, responsibility for errors, data-transfer reluctance, and prompt-formulation difficulty. It contains no reliable answers about the real last task, so it is directional only.
- Assumptions to test: the friction is tied to a real case, occurs at synthesis or validation rather than only at a generic preference level, and a saved structured artefact addresses a meaningful part of the workflow.
- Target segment and eligibility: people who, within the last 90 days, completed a multi-source task and delivered or used a structured artefact (for example a brief, analysis, plan, or presentation) and can reconstruct the last case without sharing confidential content. Exclude people whose relevant task is only imagined or older than 90 days from the real-case route.
- Interaction status: real past interaction; use a separate counterfactual route only for people without an eligible case.
- Consent and data-retention boundary: before the session, state the research purpose, voluntary participation, right to skip or stop, and that no names, contacts, raw recordings, confidential source materials, or identifiable quotations are retained in the harness. Record only consented aggregate notes labelled `real past case` or `counterfactual`.

## Method and decision rule

- Method and artefact: 5–7 consented semi-structured interviews, 20–30 minutes each. Use the two question routes below; retain an aggregate stage-by-stage barrier table, not raw transcripts.
- Supporting signal: at least 3 of 5 eligible real-case participants independently describe a consequential synthesis or validation barrier, name a workaround, and connect it to time, rework, uncertainty, or delayed decision; at least 2 say that an explicit structured-artifact plus validation step would fit the reconstructed workflow, subject to a later observed-prototype test.
- Disconfirming signal: fewer than 3 of 5 eligible real-case participants identify such a barrier, or the barrier is mainly elsewhere in the journey, or participants consistently reject the proposed fit because it would add work, fail data constraints, or not help their actual outcome.
- Minimum evidence required to decide: five eligible real-case reconstructions or an explicit `NOT_RUN` at sprint close. Counterfactual responses never count toward confirmation.
- What this method cannot establish: product usability, willingness to pay, comparative advantage, or actual task-time reduction. Those require observed prototype use or later outcome testing.

## Interview guide

### Route A — real past experience

1. Think of the most recent qualifying task. What triggered it, what outcome did you need, and what was delivered or decided?
2. Please walk me through what actually happened: finding sources, choosing them, reading or extracting information, synthesising it, validating it, and making the final decision.
3. At which exact step did time, uncertainty, or rework increase? What made it difficult?
4. What did you do instead or as a workaround? What signal tells you that it cost effort or affected the result?
5. How did you check the result before relying on it? What remained uncertain or had to be redone?
6. If you could change just one step in this completed task, which one and why?

### Route B — ideal or imagined scenario

1. If you needed to complete a comparable task in the future, what final result would be useful?
2. What would have to be true for you to try a flow that helps structure information and validate an artefact?
3. Where would that flow need to fit into your current process, and what would make you avoid it?
4. What constraints would matter: confidentiality, responsibility for errors, time, existing tools, or something else?

> Label all Route B statements as `counterfactual expectation`; do not report them as observed barriers or confirmation of the hypothesis.
