# Pilot desk research

## Trigger

Use before recruitment, interview planning, or a pilot-user decision when existing evidence may help frame a question. It does not validate a product hypothesis by itself.

## Inputs

- Decision or hypothesis to inform
- Target user, market, and time boundary
- Available sources and a bounded timebox

## Procedure

1. Start from `templates/desk-research.md`; state the decision before searching.
2. Collect only evidence that can answer a stated question. For each item, record the source, publication date, evidence type, and limitation.
3. Seek a counterpoint or a source that could falsify the leading interpretation.
4. Separate sourced findings, assumptions, and open field questions. Dated or low-quality evidence remains a limitation, not a fact.
5. State whether interviews, observation, or a prototype test are still required, then link the brief from the relevant pilot task.
6. Update `state.md`, `decisions.md`, or `risks.md` only when the evidence changes a durable plan, decision, or risk.

## Boundaries

- Do not treat AI output, a search snippet, or a single secondary source as evidence.
- Do not collect participant data, contact people, or publish research without owner confirmation.
- Do not copy long passages from sources; preserve links, concise findings, and limitations instead.

## Done

The brief names the decision, evidence boundary, source table, contradictions, limitations, and next validation method.

## Ownership and revision

The project owner reviews the brief before it informs outreach or a product decision. Revise when the decision, target segment, or material evidence changes.
