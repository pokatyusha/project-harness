# Monetization and unit model

## Trigger

Use when Direction 2.5 scope or a defence business-model section needs to explain how value could be created, captured, and measured. It frames a model; it does not validate pricing, demand, or profitability.

## Inputs

- Product value proposition, target user or customer, and known alternatives
- Evidence for pricing, costs, use, or market constraints
- Decision the model is meant to inform

## Procedure

1. Start from `templates/monetization-unit-model.md` and state the decision and evidence boundary.
2. Describe value creation, delivery, and capture separately; mark an unstated business model as an assumption.
3. Name the proposed revenue mechanism and why its unit matches the value exchange: for example, customer, transaction, contract, or resource consumption.
4. List the required revenue, direct-cost, acquisition, retention, and capacity inputs without substituting missing numbers.
5. State the formula or observable metric needed for the decision, along with what would invalidate the model.
6. When real, traceable inputs are available and an economic calculation is requested, hand off to `founder-cfo` or `founder-pricing`; retain their estimates as estimates.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent price, CAC, LTV, retention, cost, volume, or margin values.
- Do not present a monetization hypothesis as a business fact, financial forecast, or investment recommendation.
- Do not use a unit-economics calculation to replace verified user need, consented pilot research, or technical feasibility work.

## Done

The record states the value exchange, model assumption or evidence, chosen unit, required inputs, formula or metric, uncertainty, and next validation action.

## Ownership and revision

The product owner approves the business-model framing; financial claims require evidence review. Revise when the value exchange, revenue mechanism, or input evidence changes.
